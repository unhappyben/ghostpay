Recovery playbooks

The failures that actually happen, and the way out of each.

Dead tab before the announce

Symptom

The tab died after generating a receive address but before announcing it, and the address is gone from the screen.

Why it is fine

Every derived receive address is recorded in localStorage under gp-recv-records the moment it is generated. On reload, unannounced records surface in the RECOVERY banner with a count and the latest address. A restored watch-only session reuses the last pending record as its receive address, so an address you already shared stays alive.

Fix

Reload the app, read the address from the RECOVERY banner, share or announce it again. Records prune automatically once a payment to that address is swept: funds moved, nothing left to recover.

Dropped / phantom transaction

Symptom

The relayer returned a hash, GET /status/:hash says pending for many minutes, and explorers show nothing at all.

Why

Broadcasts go to Flashbots Protect's private mempool: invisibility on explorers is normal until mined. But Protect can also silently drop a transaction it cannot include, and (before the affordability preflight) it accepted unfunded transactions that never mined, producing phantom pendings.

Fix

Usually nothing: the relayer's reaper journals every announce and intent sweep to broadcasts.jsonl, waits a 6-minute grace window, and rebroadcasts with a fresh nonce, up to 4 attempts. Check the journal's finalStatus: confirmed, replaced (with replacedBy carrying the new hash), reverted, or given-up. Operator-side, keep the runner funded: the affordability preflight now refuses broadcasts the runner cannot pay for with a 400 instead of a phantom hash. A legacy sweep or withdrawal that never landed is not journaled; its artifact stays valid, so re-submit it.

Stale authorization

Symptom

POST /sweep fails with: stale authorization: stealth EOA nonce is N but the artifact was signed for M.

Why

The 7702 authorization is bound to the stealth EOA's nonce at signing time. Something landed on that address since (another sweep, or a rebroadcast the reaper already made), so the signed nonce no longer matches.

Fix

Re-scan and SIGN SWEEP again in the app. The fresh artifact carries the current nonce. The old artifact is dead weight; discard it.

Lost pp-secret

Symptom

The deposit is in Privacy Pools, but the pp-secret file is gone.

Why there is no fix

The sweep deposits against a poseidon2(nullifier, secret) precommitment. The secret is generated in the browser at sweep time and downloaded as a file right then; it exists nowhere else, by design. Without it no withdrawal proof can be constructed. The funds are unrecoverable. This is the one failure in this list with no playbook.

Prevention

The money-safety layer blocks further sweeps once a secret exists until you confirm the file is saved (or pass a re-upload check). The encrypted backup/restore export (PBKDF2, 600k iterations, AES-GCM) carries local state to another device. Keep copies of the secret file itself; it is the only key to the deposit.

Wrong-wallet scan

Symptom

Connected, generated, scanned: zero payments, and the meta-address on screen does not match the one that was shared.

Why

Keys derive from one wallet signature. A different wallet produces a different signature, different keys, and a scanner that finds nothing: every announcement fails the meta check.

Fix

Connect the same wallet account used when the keys were first generated, and sign again. Compare the meta-address to the one that was shared before concluding anything is lost. Watch-only sessions carry the viewing key of the original wallet, so a restored session scanning fine while a fresh connect finds nothing is the same mismatch in reverse.

Cursor ahead of history

Symptom

A payment is known to exist (the payer shows the transaction) but scans do not surface it, typically on a new device or a restored session.

Why

Incremental scans resume from the stored cursor (ghostpay:lastScanned:<viewPub>). If the cursor is ahead of the block containing the announcement, the scan never looks back far enough. A default rescan starts at latest minus 50000 blocks, which can still be too recent for an old payment.

Fix

Use RESCAN with an explicit from-block at or before the payment's block. For announcements made before 2026-09-08, also tick the legacy scheme checkbox: those used the sha256 shared-secret scheme and derive different addresses.

Relayer-side failures (journal, reaper, runner funding) are covered in Relayer.
